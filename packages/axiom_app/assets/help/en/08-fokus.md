# Focus, stimulation and the brake

Three tools on one row below the main view. They belong together: concentrated work earns stimulation budget, and an uncovered need for stimulation is the most common reason the brake gets used.

## Focus

A block has a target and a length — 15, 25, 50, 75 or 90 minutes. Under raised load the longer options are not selectable, see [State](kapitel:05).

The governor is deliberately asymmetric: it **protects** the block and interrupts only with a reason it can show. A badly timed interruption destroys the most valuable state this profile has — it costs more than any missed interruption brings in.

![A running block, twelve minutes over the planned time, and the governor's verdict.](img/fokus.webp)

Four verdicts are possible: **PROTECTED**, **NOTE**, **INTERRUPTION** and **STOP NOW**. An appointment always beats focus. Without a target set, AXIOM asks once quietly after 45 minutes whether this is still the right thing.

When you end a block it asks for one sentence: where did you leave off? That is the actual purpose of the exit — without that note, loading the context starts from scratch next time.

## Stimulation

The hunger for stimulation is a need, not a fault. It cannot be trained away; left uncovered it finds the fastest channel, and the fastest is almost always the most expensive.

![Stimulation need, earned budget, and a suggestion from your own channels.](img/reiz.webp)

You define the channels yourself — with intensity, typical length, and whether the channel costs something. Costly channels are not forbidden, only never suggested on their own.

Concentrated work earns budget at a rate of 1:3: ninety minutes of obligation unlock thirty minutes. A slot works without credit too — it is just counted differently. Unplanned slots get counted, not punished.

## The brake

A trigger is an action you want to take in the moment and often no longer want the next day. AXIOM does not block it. It puts waiting time in between: 5, 15, 30 or 60 minutes, 24 hours, or until 09:00.

![The wait is running. "I'll do it" only unlocks once it is over.](img/bremse.webp)

You write the check questions yourself, while calm. No trigger exists without at least one question — the question is the contract. Somebody else's question gets clicked away; your own gets answered.

"I'll skip it" works immediately. "I'll do it" unlocks only after the wait, and gets counted, not judged. How often a trigger has held is shown on it later.
