# Time anchors

An appointment at 14:00 does not cost sixty minutes, it costs a hundred and fifty. So AXIOM counts backwards — from arrival to the moment you have to stop doing whatever you are doing.

In this profile punctuality is usually compensated successfully, and that is precisely where the cost sits: buffers, constant recalculation, background tension before every appointment. An entered anchor moves that calculation out of your head and into the system.

## What it asks for

Not just the time, but four quantities:

| Input | Default |
|---|---|
| Travel | 20 min |
| Getting ready | 15 min |
| Buffer | 10 min |
| Getting out | 10 min |

**Getting out** is the step the head always forgets. Coming out of whatever is currently running takes time — and without those minutes you either arrive late or tear yourself out of the depth you were in.

![Two anchors with their full chain. The lead time stands next to each one.](img/anker.webp)

## The chain

Out of the four inputs comes a chain of fixed times, counted backwards:

- 14:00 dentist
- 13:25 leave for Praxis Bergmann
- 13:10 get ready
- 13:00 wrap up what is running

Every step gets an exact alarm. The last one in the list is the first one to arrive — and the only one that actually prevents anything.

While you change the inputs, the chain is already there underneath. You see immediately what five more minutes of buffer mean.

## On the main view

The next due step sits at the very top, above everything else: it is the only thing on that screen that gets more expensive by waiting. Below twenty minutes remaining, the strip is highlighted.

An appointment also beats any running focus block — see [Focus, stimulation and the brake](kapitel:08).

## What anchors are not

Not a calendar and not a calendar replacement. AXIOM reads no calendar and manages no invitations. An anchor is an appointment whose *lead time* you want offloaded — for everything else the calendar you already have is enough.

Accuracy to the minute requires the exact-alarm permission. Whether it has been granted is shown under *System → System check*.
